DATA BENDER: TIME, FREEZE, MEMORY AND LONG-RUN VINYL CHARACTER
2 October 2026 — final focused investigation before module specifications

CONCLUSION
==========
There is musically significant behavior here beyond ordinary frozen looping.
Freeze does not necessarily preserve an immutable snapshot of everything a
later Time setting can play. The reader remains anchored in shared memory,
its playable extent changes, and a history writer continues recording outside
an evolving boundary. Changing Time can therefore reveal another captured bank,
reveal incoming audio recorded AFTER freezing, or revisit material subsequently
overwritten by the history writer.

This is a useful candidate for an intentional Rack feature: frozen playback
with explorable/evolving surrounding memory. A conventional locked snapshot
would remove that behavior. We should specify the distinction explicitly.

Vinyl's recovered model also holds up past natural slow-modulation rollovers
and through deliberately exercised rare events. Its character is a structured
mixture of held pulses, correlated noise, and bass attenuation. The slow
modulation belongs to the generated noise; this routine does not implement
pitch wow/flutter by moving a playback head.

1. WHAT WAS RUN, AND WHAT WAS NOT
================================
New scripts/results live in analysis/character/:

  probe_time_memory.py / time_memory_probes.json
    Ten 76,800-stereo-frame trajectories using the original Buffer processor.
    Expand, shrink and restore; forward +1, reverse -1 and slow +0.125;
    frozen banks at two initial anchors and unfrozen comparisons. A tenth
    case disables outer clock requests to isolate their effect on slow reads.

  probe_history_boundary.py / history_boundary_probes.json
    Two 96-frame diagnostic cases near a history-ring wrap, with active Freeze.
    Tests whether a retained read-bank anchor can overlap the history writer.

  probe_vinyl_long.py / vinyl_long_probes.json
    Three 288,096-frame comparisons against the independent Vinyl model:
    amounts .25 and 1, plus amount 1 with a 192-frame interval at the .05 bypass
    threshold. Natural modulation rollovers; original initializer, libm and
    noise-generator instructions, without substituted DSP or RNG routines.

  probe_vinyl_rare.py / vinyl_rare_probes.json
    Five 31-frame comparisons, deliberately preparing RNG/counter states to
    exercise a nonzero event in each of the five dust layers. Nonzero stereo
    input is supplied here; the long-run cases use silence to expose texture.

  vinyl_silence_48k.wav
    Original firmware Vinyl output on silence, amount 1, seed 1, 6.002 seconds
    at an assumed 48 kHz playback rate. Fixed gain .5, 16-bit stereo PCM;
    no peak normalization. This is an isolated DSP audition, not board audio.

The Time experiments use the pre-existing buffer harness and its stated
rand/powf/memset substitutions. They retain 96-frame channel-major processing.
They use a reduced capacity of 32,768 frames per channel to exercise memory
reuse quickly. This is NOT the hardware's 3,601,050-frame channel capacity.
Consequently, observed history-turnover times are not hardware durations.

The buffer Time guard is driven by a documented float32 model of the callback's
period smoother and 5 ms condition. Clock requests come from a fractional phase
model delivered as a pending block flag. The original Buffer instructions run;
the complete hardware callback, UI polling, external edge estimator, Corrupt
and output path do not run in these Time experiments. The driver is an explicit
controlled stimulus, not a claim of sample-identical physical knob gestures.

2. FROZEN MEMORY: THE ANCHOR CAN STAY WHILE ITS EXTENT CHANGES
============================================================
Relevant original routines:
  0x08004d58 Buffer processor / resize acceptance / auxiliary history writer
  0x080047a8 Capture writer
  0x0800493c Fractional reader / bank choice

Initial capture length N=2400. Two prepared read anchors are 0 and 2400.
Both planes begin with address-coded, alternating-sign samples below .6 in
magnitude. Incoming audio has distinct magnitudes .71, .75, .8 and .85 across
the four stages. This distinguishes retained initial samples from fresh input
without interpreting waveform correlations. Window and Corrupt are absent.

Trajectory: baseline N=2400 -> target 4800 -> target 1200 -> target 2400.
The settled lengths in this driver are 2400, 4799, 1200, 2399 due to the original
frequency/reciprocal and integer arithmetic. These one-frame differences are
recorded, rather than silently rounded away in the analysis.

All frozen cases retain their initial read-bank anchor through the trajectory.
Changing N does not automatically relocate a frozen reader to a newly organized
independent frozen buffer. It changes the region accessible from that anchor.

Frozen, forward, read anchor 0:
- Baseline reads only initial bank 0.
- Expansion reads initial banks 0 AND 1. The original 4,800-frame region remains
  unchanged, but the reader can now visit more of it.
- Shrink returns playback to a smaller portion of initial bank 0.

Frozen, forward, read anchor 2400:
- Baseline reads initial bank 1.
- Expansion reaches addresses 2400..7198, including the original history area
  beginning at 4800. In the expansion stage, 11,340 of 24,576 left reader calls
  sample freshly recorded history rather than initial frozen material.
- Shrink reduces the accessible region. Restore later reads a mixture of
  surviving initial bank-1 samples and newly written samples at that same
  original bank-1 address range.

These are read-index/provenance counts, not audio mixing proportions: the
linear reader also accesses its neighboring interpolation sample. Fresh data
was supplied after Freeze was already active. The experiment therefore directly
establishes the possibility of exposing post-Freeze material in this setup.

Design consequence: decide what Time edits mean while frozen. Useful explicit
options are (a) an immutable captured take, or (b) a stable playback anchor with
explorable surrounding live history. The second is closer to the interesting
behavior above, without requiring accidental memory-layout dependencies.

3. SHRINK/RESTORE IS NOT GUARANTEED TO RECOVER AN UNCHANGED TAKE
=============================================================
The auxiliary history writer's start boundary is based on approximately 2*N,
clamped to capacity. Its cursor continues where it is while still within the
allowed range; on wrap it returns to the CURRENT boundary. It does not always
jump to a smaller boundary immediately when Time shrinks.

Thus two things matter independently:
- Which addresses become eligible for future history writes.
- When the advancing cursor actually reaches those addresses.

In the frozen forward/anchor-2400 trajectory, 204 original bank-region samples
change during the restore stage, and 1,807 left reader calls in that stage read
fresh values from original bank 1. In the reverse counterpart, 1,018 samples
change and 9,643 left reader calls read fresh bank-1 values. These are fixture
results, not universal percentages or device-rate timing claims. Resize
acceptance depends on read crossings, so direction changes memory evolution too.

The smaller targeted experiment removes ambiguity about the mechanism:
- Freeze is active; current N=1200; history starts at 2400.
- The injected history cursor begins at 32766, just before the reduced plane's
  end. Both channels run 96 frames with input .8 and old memory .2.
- History wraps and replaces addresses 2400..2493: 94 samples.
- With read anchor 0, playback remains .2.
- With retained read anchor 2400 and speed .125, playback becomes .575 on frame
  3, then .8 on frame 4, while active Freeze remains true. The intermediate
  value is linear interpolation across the advancing replacement boundary.

This is a prepared reachable-mechanism test, not a simulated complete gesture
sequence from hardware boot. The larger trajectories separately demonstrate
that frozen anchors can persist across resizing and fresh material can be read.

Specification implication: a plain 'Freeze stops recording' Boolean loses
this interaction. A better explicit model separates capture protection,
playback anchoring, history recording and history-retention policy. We can make
the exploration controllable and repeatable instead of preserving surprising
read/write overlap as an undocumented rule.

4. TIME, DIRECTION AND SLOW PLAYBACK
===================================
Changing Time does not automatically change the signed rate in these tests.
All recorded rate states remain +1, -1 or +.125 as selected. The main effect is
changing extent and accepted boundaries, with direction-dependent read/write
ordering and resets. It is not equivalent to a conventional delay-time control
that necessarily drags a read head through the recording to produce a smooth
pitch bend.

Reverse reaches the newly included portion from a different edge and accepts
resizes at different samples. Its shrink stage can continue briefly through
material outside the eventually settled shorter region. Those discontinuities
and changes in fragment order are candidates for the characteristic sound;
we should not replace every boundary with a long smoothing operation.

Slow playback exposes an additional scheduling interaction. For the frozen
anchor-2400, speed .125 case:

  Expanded stage with supplied clock requests:
    24,576 left reader calls visit initial bank 1; none visit fresh history.

  Same trajectory with outer clock requests disabled:
    16,128 visit initial bank 1; 8,448 visit fresh history.
    Read indices reach 5855 rather than remaining in the shorter visited range.

The clock requests can repeatedly accept phase resets before a slow reader
traverses the whole expanded region. This is not a different speed mapping.
It changes WHICH fragment can be heard at that speed. The comparisons isolate
outer requests; other internal writer/reader behavior remains in the engine.

Specification implication: pitch, slice duration and reset cadence should be
separate concepts, even if the default controls couple them. A clocked retrigger
mode and a freely traversing frozen mode offer meaningfully different musical
results. Preserve a mode that repeatedly slows/reverses fragments inside a
clocked phrase; simply letting every slowed loop finish would lose that effect.

5. VINYL: LONG-RUN MODEL VERIFIED, INCLUDING PAUSE/RESUME
=======================================================
Across the three natural runs, every one of 1,728,576 interleaved output floats
matched the independent model bit for bit. The modulation counter, short noise
counter, modulation value and held slow-noise value also matched at every
96-frame block boundary. This extends the previous fresh 128-frame coverage.

With continuous enabled processing, modulation updates at zero-based frames:
  48013, 96027, 144041, 192055, 240069, 288083.

The period is 48,014 enabled stereo frames: floor(.5 * F_init), with
F_init=96028. At an assumed 48 kHz execution rate, that is about 1.00029 seconds,
not half a physical second. Actual hardware rate still requires measurement.

The first three modulation values are:
  -0.0814135671, +0.1109498665, +0.0890981555.

The initial modulation is zero. The slower held-noise contribution is therefore
absent until the first update; hiss and dust are already running. Once enabled,
the held noise changes every ten processed samples and its scale changes at
the much slower modulation boundary. This is noise-amplitude behavior, not an
input playback-speed oscillator.

The pause test sets amount to exactly .05 for frames 47904..48095. Processing
bypasses and the sample-processing states stop advancing. The first modulation
update moves to frame 48205, exactly 192 later; subsequent updates shift by the
same amount. It resumes its prior sequence rather than generating a new seed.
Parameter calculations can still run while bypassed; the observed pause is in
the sample-processing states, not a claim that the whole object is untouched.

Specification implication: retain a zero/low-amount bypass zone and define
whether animated texture pauses, continues silently, or resets on re-entry.
The original pause/resume is now experimentally supported, not just inferred.

6. VINYL: RARE PULSES AND STEREO STRUCTURE ARE WORTH PRESERVING
=============================================================
Natural nonzero pulse counts in 288,096 enabled frames, explicit seed 1:

Amount      D0       D1       D2       D3       D4
.25         61       57        0        0        1
1.0        407      369        3        0        2

These are counts for one deterministic realization, not estimated universal
rates. D3 produced no event in either natural run. Five additional prepared
RNG-state tests explicitly exercised every dust layer, including D3, and matched
all outputs and dust-state values against the model with zero numerical error.

Important texture decisions established by the code and exercised here:
- D0/D1 hold a selected pulse for five samples; D2/D3/D4 hold it for fifteen.
  They are short rectangles before their respective routing/filtering, not
  isolated one-sample clicks or arbitrarily shaped exponential transients.
- D0 and D1 join shared hiss and shared slow noise, pass through separate noise
  highpasses, and crossfeed at .3.
- The larger D2 and D3 pulses are channel-specific. D4 is added to both outputs.
- The large pulses enter after the noise highpasses, so they retain a different
  shape/spectrum from the frequent fine crackle.
- Increasing the main amount simultaneously changes noise density, pulse/noise
  amplitudes, input highpass cutoff and input gain. It is a composed voicing,
  not just a wet/dry blend for a fixed crackle generator.

At .25 the silence-output peak/RMS are approximately .3160/.002989; at 1 they
are .8207/.01462 in these runs. Those ratios illustrate sparse peaks over a
quiet floor. They are internal float amplitudes, not Rack voltages or a claim
about physical headroom. The supplied audition file uses fixed attenuation .5.

For a native-rate design, preserve event rate and pulse duration independently.
For a dust layer updated every h reference frames:
  expected events/second = (Fs_execution / h) * density / F_init

Replacing that with a per-host-sample density test without accounting for h
would make the texture much denser. Likewise, keeping exactly five/fifteen host
samples at every host rate changes pulse duration and spectrum. A fixed-rate
texture core is one simple option; an event scheduler with explicit durations
is another. Preserve the stereo common/private components in either approach.

Wow/flutter can be a useful added feature, but should be separately voiced.
Adding pitch wobble merely because this effect is named Vinyl would not follow
the recovered algorithm and could obscure the texture we are trying to retain.

7. WHAT THIS CHANGES IN THE UPCOMING MODULE SPEC
===============================================
I would now carry these requirements into specification work:

1. Offer frozen playback that can explore surrounding memory. Define a stable
   anchor, eligible history, retention duration and what shrink/expand does.
   Decide whether an immutable snapshot is another mode or the primary mode.

2. Keep clocked fragment retriggering distinct from free traversal. Slow and
   reverse playback should support both, with the default retaining the
   relationship between clock, capture length and repeated fragments.

3. Preserve the gritty transition option. Safer stereo coordination and correct
   clock timestamps need not erase cuts, short repeats, or direction-dependent
   phrase rearrangement. Make transition smoothing a texture choice.

4. Preserve Vinyl's layered pulse/noise composition and correlated stereo
   routing. Its irregular pulses, state continuity, bypass threshold and
   coupled amount mapping deserve a reference voicing before we add controls.

5. Define rates in explicit units. Separate design coefficients, renderer rate,
   pulse duration, event density and modulation period. Do not silently change
   all of them by substituting host sample rate for the constant 96028.

The evidence is sufficient to move to specifications. Exact full callback/UI
integration, physical rate/analog response and natural boot randomness remain
open, but are no longer prerequisites for describing the musical behaviors we
want. Before promising hardware-exact Freeze gestures, use full integration or
board captures: these tests intentionally isolate the underlying mechanism.
