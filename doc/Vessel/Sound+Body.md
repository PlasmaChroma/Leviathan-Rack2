Physiology tells us the human **ear and skull** act as resonant cavities: the ear canal naturally amplifies frequencies in the speech range (roughly 2–5 kHz), while bone conduction enhances the lower spectrum (below a few hundred Hz).  For example, intense infrasound near 18.9 Hz – the resonant frequency of the human eyeball – can literally make vision blur and induce strange “ghostly” sensations.  In short, different tissues and cavities have preferred frequencies: eyes ~19 Hz, middle ear ~3–4 kHz, etc.  Whole-body vibratory studies confirm this: low-frequency (<30 Hz) oscillations travel deep into trunk and limbs, affecting proprioception and muscle tone, whereas higher tones feel more surface-level. 

In **clinical and experimental research**, specific bands have been linked to distinct effects.  Whole-body vibration therapy often uses ~20–50 Hz to train muscles and bone.  For stroke rehabilitation, protocols use roughly *20–30 Hz vibrations* (2–4 mm amplitude) to reduce spasticity and rebuild muscle control.  Similarly, patients with spinal injuries respond best around *45 Hz* for lower-limb muscle activation.  In elders, higher frequencies (>25 Hz) tend to maximize balance and strength gains.  Conversely, vibroacoustic stress-reduction uses even lower bass: one study placed participants on a vibro-bed pulsing at 0–100 Hz alongside gentle music and found a marked **increase in parasympathetic tone (relaxation)** compared to music alone.  (Another trial with nature sounds found no effect unless music was added, hinting that complex auditory context matters.)  In brief, 20–40 Hz inputs often modulate **muscles, bone and balance**, while the very lowest bass (0–20 Hz) taps into autonomic nerves and even internal organs, relaxing the system.

**Synthesis: Frequency ↦ Body-Part Map.**  Combining strands, we sketch a composite chart: 

- **<30 Hz (infrasound/vibration):** Chest cavity and organs (heart, lungs, gut) and vestibular/ocular systems.  Intense infrasound (~1–20 Hz) can vibrate the abdomen and eyeballs, triggering nausea or vertigo.  In vibro-therapy, ~20–30 Hz relaxes spastic muscles via spinal reflexes.  

- **30–50 Hz:** Musculoskeletal system (spine, legs, core).  Common in vibration rehab: *~20–30 Hz* boosts muscle tone and circulation, *45 Hz* powerfully activates leg muscles in SCI patients, and high 30s Hz improve balance in elders.  These frequencies often hit bones and joints, stimulating proprioceptors and even osteocytes (affecting bone density).

- **50–100 Hz:** Larger muscle groups and some neural rhythms.  This band straddles muscle/mechano-receptor peaks (Pacinian corpuscles respond up here).  Vibration around 60–80 Hz, while not widely studied for specific organs, is close to motor unit firing rates and might entrain neuromuscular circuits.  (For instance, therapeutic devices often use 60 Hz tones for deep tissue massage.)  

- **100–300 Hz:** Organs & glands.  Human speaking fundamentals (~100–250 Hz for male voices, up to ~300 Hz for female voices) naturally resonate the throat cavity and chest.  Frequencies here couple into viscera: early research suggests kidneys and gut tissues absorb more energy at low hundreds of Hz.  Some acoustic therapies (e.g. Tibetan bowls at 110–240 Hz) target the *solar plexus* (stomach) and *throat* regions.  Notably, one 174 Hz solfeggio tone is said to aid pain relief – possibly because 174 Hz lies near the body's mechanoreceptor sweet-spot.

- **300–600 Hz:** Brainstem and nervous pathways.  Low mid-range sound stimulates the head and cranial nerves.  The ear’s own resonance (~2–5 kHz) hasn’t fully kicked in yet, but frequencies of a few hundred Hz can vibrate the skull and modulate brainstem auditory nuclei.  (In fact, some “binaural beat” therapies use carrier frequencies in this range to entrain brainwave patterns.)  Anecdotally, 396 Hz (root chakra) is linked to grounding, and 417 Hz to facilitating change – perhaps corresponding to pelvic and abdominal organs.

- **600–1000 Hz:** Central nervous system and pineal.  Approaching the upper hearing range of many mammals, sounds around 741–852 Hz are said to cleanse the body and align spiritual order.  In practice, these tones resonate the upper skull and pituitary region.  Interestingly, 963 Hz (added solfeggio) is claimed to activate the pineal gland and unity, suggesting a 1 kHz-ish frequency engaging deep midbrain centers.

- **1–2 kHz:** Auditory & emotional centers.  This high range is fully in the realm of the **audible peak** (the ear canal actually peaks around 2–3 kHz).  Such frequencies most strongly excite cochlear hair cells and thus the auditory cortex.  Music in this range tends to grab attention and emotion (think crying violin or human screams).  No specific organ “map” here, beyond the ear itself, but we can say ~1 kHz waves powerfully engage neural circuits in the brain and stress centers (given that human screams around 1 kHz are physiologically alarming).

Each frequency band thus “lights up” different tissues: **sub-30 Hz** shakes the core and vision, **tens of Hz** buffet muscles and bones, **hundreds of Hz** tickle organs and cranial structures, and **above 1 kHz** fully occupies the auditory and emotional brain.  Traditional chakra charts weave a similar path up the torso (root at low, crown at high frequencies), while modern studies hint at partial resonances (e.g. eye at ~19 Hz, stress systems at 528 Hz).
